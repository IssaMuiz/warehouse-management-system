# WMS Business Rules and Transaction Lifecycle

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the initial operational rules for production receipts, sales, dispatches, transfers and stock control.

These rules are provisional until verified against the company's actual procedures, documents and approval requirements.

## 2. Core Principles

1. Physical stock and recorded stock must be traceable.
2. Stock must not be increased or decreased by directly editing a balance.
3. Every posted stock movement must have a source reference and audit history.
4. Draft or pending transactions must not affect available stock unless a specifically approved reservation mechanism is introduced.
5. Repeated submissions must not create duplicate transactions.
6. Posted transactions must be corrected through controlled reversals or adjustments rather than silently overwritten.
7. Users may perform only the operations permitted by their company, role and warehouse assignments.

## 3. Production Recording and Receipt

### 3.1 Production recording

The production manager records:
- Production reference.
- Filling sheet number.
- Product code.
- Batch number.
- Produced quantity.
- Production date.

Each filling sheet represents exactly one product and one batch number.

### 3.2 Warehouse receipt

1. The receiving officer searches for the filling sheet number.
2. The system retrieves the corresponding production record.
3. The officer checks the product, batch and expected quantity.
4. The officer enters the actual quantity physically received.
5. The system validates the receipt and records any discrepancy.
6. Only the quantity accepted under the approved receiving rules is posted into warehouse stock.

Recording production does not itself increase warehouse stock.

The filling sheet and receipt must be protected against duplicate processing.

## 4. Customer-Service Orders and Dispatch

### 4.1 Approved order import

1. Customer-service orders are imported from an approved spreadsheet format.
2. Each approval number must originate from the source system or authorised customer-service record.
3. Reimporting a previously processed file must not create duplicate orders.
4. Invalid rows must be reported clearly without silently changing their values.
5. The import process must preserve source references for traceability.

### 4.2 Dispatch

1. The dispatch officer selects the appropriate warehouse.
2. The officer searches for the customer and approval number.
3. The system retrieves the approved order lines and relevant quantities.
4. The officer enters the actual invoice number generated by the company's invoicing process.
5. The officer confirms the quantities actually dispatched.
6. The system validates the order, warehouse stock, permissions and fulfilment rules.
7. A successful dispatch deducts the posted dispatched quantities from stock and records the transaction.

Approval numbers and actual invoice numbers are separate references.

### 4.3 Partial fulfilment and cancellation

Partial fulfilment, cancellation, order amendment and reservation behaviour must follow the company's verified procedures.

The system must prevent cumulative fulfilment from exceeding the authorised order quantity unless a documented and authorised amendment permits it.

## 5. Inter-Warehouse Transfers

### 5.1 Transfer dispatch

1. An authorised user initiates a transfer between warehouses.
2. The transfer follows the company's required approval rules.
3. The sending warehouse confirms the quantities dispatched.
4. The system deducts dispatched quantities from the sending warehouse and records them as in transit.

### 5.2 Transfer receipt

1. The destination warehouse retrieves the transfer using its original reference.
2. The receiving officer compares dispatched and physically received quantities.
3. The officer records actual received quantities and any discrepancy.
4. The accepted quantity is posted into destination stock.
5. Unreceived quantities remain accounted for as in transit or as a documented exception until resolved under approved rules.

A transfer is not a sale and must not create sales revenue.

Transfer references and receipt submissions must be protected against duplicate processing.

## 6. Sales and Stock Movements

Sales must be recorded against the appropriate customer category:
- Retail.
- Reseller.
- Industrial.

A sale must retain its invoice reference, warehouse, customer, product lines, quantities and other required business fields.

Only a successfully posted dispatch may deduct stock.

The system must not allow an ordinary sales transaction to consume stock from an unauthorised warehouse.

## 7. Discrepancies, Damage and Adjustments

The system must support the reporting and investigation of:
- Production quantity discrepancies.
- Transfer dispatch-versus-receipt discrepancies.
- Damaged products.
- Physical stock count differences.
- Other approved stock exceptions.

The exact approval, quarantine, write-off and adjustment procedures must be confirmed with the company before implementation.

Reporting a discrepancy must not silently change stock.

## 8. Daily Warehouse Reporting

Each warehouse must be able to submit its required daily operational report.

A report must explicitly distinguish:
- Activity occurred.
- No activity occurred.
- Report not yet submitted.

A missing report must never be interpreted as a zero-activity report.

The system should support consolidated reporting for authorised management users.

## 9. Stock Ledger and Audit History

Every posted stock movement must record, as applicable:
- Company and warehouse.
- Product and batch.
- Quantity and movement direction.
- Transaction type.
- Source document and reference.
- Posting timestamp.
- User or trusted system identity.
- Reversal or correction relationship, where applicable.

The system must support reconciliation between stock movements and stock balances.

## 10. Multi-Company Access

The platform must support multiple companies.

Company administrators manage their own company's users, warehouses and products.

Warehouse staff are restricted to their assigned warehouses and authorised functions.

Platform administration and company administration are separate permission levels.

All permissions must be enforced by trusted backend logic, not merely by hiding interface elements.

## 11. Rules Requiring Business Confirmation

Before implementation of the affected modules, confirm:
- Whether stock is reserved when an order is approved.
- Whether dispatch quantities can differ from approved quantities.
- How multiple invoices or dispatches relate to one approval number.
- The rules for cancelling or amending approved orders.
- Who may initiate and approve transfers.
- How short, excess or damaged transfer receipts are resolved.
- Whether batches must be selected for every dispatch.
- How damaged stock is quarantined and written off.
- Which users may post adjustments and approve reversals.
- The required fields and deadlines for daily reports.

## 12. Change Control

When a business rule is confirmed or changed:
1. Update this document.
2. Record the decision and its source where appropriate.
3. Assess its impact on database design, security and workflows.
4. Add or update test cases.
5. Commit the change to GitHub before implementing the affected behaviour.
